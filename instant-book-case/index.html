The problem

Pros didn't just complain. They started leaving.

My role

  • PM, Hire team, owner of the instant book experience
  • Initial goal: improve instant book hire rate and instant booking share → realize the transition to the new fulfillment model

What January showed

  • Pros disliked instant book: churn, and reduced bids on leads · sustained additional support volume
  • Customers with the option to instant book monetizing worse than expected
  • Overall ~−4% revenue drag, with indications it would get worse

My goals said "improve and widen." The situation demanded two questions:

  • 1. What do we do short term to stem the revenue impact?
  • 2. Could we actually realize the vision of 100% of projects instant booked, at revenue neutrality, and at what booking share?
Task 1 · Short term

What do we do short term to stem the revenue impact?

Objective

Recover revenue quickly and take pressure off pros, while keeping the instant book bet alive.

Factors

  • Fast to execute: bids were repricing under us
  • Must recover revenue, not just volume
  • Still believed in IB: customer experience and repeat were positive

Options

  • Turn instant book off: abandons the bet on one quarter of data
  • Make it optional for pros: adverse selection; booking stops being a promise
  • Complex price changes: too slow to matter
  • Throttle volume ✓: fast, reversible, buys time to diagnose

Decision

  • Instant book option shown to 25% of customers
  • Plus a refund for bookings canceled quickly
  • The standard lead flow monetized well · lower instant book volume mollified pros

Result

−4% → ~−1%revenue per visitor, recoveredsupport complaints down · revisit at end of half

Task 2 · Could we realize the 100% instant booking vision?

Step 2: Where to invest, and what would it prove?

Options to invest in

  • Pricing: accuracy, quote sheets · Availability: calendars, arrival windows · Customer experience: the path into booking · …

Constraints

  • Hold revenue · deliver a step change in hire rate, not increments · protect booking share

What we decided we wanted to answer

  • Could we deliver a step change improvement in booking quality while maintaining a path to expansion? (revenue neutral · reasonable booking share)
  • Target: ~85% hire rate, roughly sized from removing the availability and pricing issues

The answer to Task 2

Instant book works in a very limited share of the marketplace

Learnings from the half

  • Instant book works where scope, price, and schedule can be trusted; in most of the marketplace those conditions fail
  • Forcing it further carries a real revenue cost
  • Getting further required heavy investment in quote sheets and availability

Returning to the core user problems

  • Any next bet had to be rooted in the evergreen problems: customers want a trustworthy pro, a fair price, fast resolution; pros want volume without wasted spend
  • Go deeper into the transaction · stay differentiated as a product vs public comps
OptionThe honest case

Revert to leads, optimizeSafest revenue; abandons differentiation and the repeat headroom
Book after connecting ✓"Connect to Book": keep the booking destination, let the conversation come first
The options I surfaced

Connect to Book: let the conversation come first and design incentives, not mandates

Why it could win

  • Headroom on customer repeat and experience: booking stays the destination
  • Pros were already paying for unresponsive customers; this aligns payment with outcomes
  • The calendar problem, solved with a carrot: pros wouldn't adopt calendars and we couldn't force them, so move the chargepoint to scheduling, making the calendar the thing that gets you paid
  • No customer contact info shared until booking, making it required to use the calendar to win a customer

The evidence

  • We surveyed pros on the options: they preferred Connect to Book. The aggrieved side of the market endorsed the pivot.

Ownership, plainly

  • Surfaced the options for the Fulfillment pillar leads: the survey, the headroom case, the options analysis
  • The decision was my skip's to make. My job was to make sure it was an informed one.

Making it real

A complete change of the hiring mechanic, sold team by team

The scope

  • Monetization changes: the unit of monetization moves to bookings
  • Telephony added to the product: calls belong on platform too
  • Communication kept on platform, enforced
  • Hiring experience changes across the funnel

Building support

  • Leadership roadshow first: the case, the survey, the headroom math
  • Then scoping and planning meetings team by team: teams upstream building with booking as a given; monetization pivoting to booking first
  • H1 deliverable: final scope and the H2 plan, with teams committed
Where it landed

Both tasks answered, and the answer became the plan

Results

  • Task 1: revenue recovered −4% → ~−1%; support down; pros mollified
  • Task 2: 100% instant booking wasn't achievable at acceptable cost; hire rate 67→75 proved the mechanics, the ~85% bar proved the limit
  • Shipped along the way: the honest booking fork · refunds · the instrumentation that made diagnosis possible
  • The pivot decision made, with the H2 scope and the plan across teams delivered on time

Reflections

  • Quality revenue ≠ quantity revenue: we only defended the revenue that was real
  • Design the escape hatch before the commitment gate
  • Incentives beat mandates: the chargepoint carrot over the calendar stick
  • And the humility: the market later tested every one of these answers
