    Design notes — why it's built this way

    1 · The ladder is the landing page. Free work needs a legible prize: both milestones, the unlock, and the weekly allowance are always on screen. Approval proves you read the bar; pickup proves the person is real and reachable — count-gaming doesn't ladder.
    2 · Roles come pre-matched from signup taxonomy. The recruiter's stated niches route them to roles where their judgment is most testable — and nomination coverage gaps steer effort to under-served roles.
    3 · Nomination ≠ outreach. No candidate is contacted by nominating. Protects candidates and Paraform's brand from unvetted outreach; the relationship starts only when a proven recruiter picks the nominee up.
    4 · Connectors is the pressure valve. Network-rich people who don't want to audition flow to referrals; nominations that fit no role also route there. The two programs share payout rails — no lead is wasted, no one is dead-ended.
    5 · The activity feed is the retention loop. Verdicts land in minutes (come back today); pickups land in days (come back this week). The feed is why a waitlisted recruiter returns without email nagging.
    6 · Provenance pays. Nominators earn a finder's reward on placement, Connectors-style — the work is compensated on outcomes even if the nominator never becomes a recruiter. Ownership stays with the engaging recruiter; credit stays with the finder.
    7 · Flagged ≠ punished. A gap verdict coaches (citing the role's real rejection reasons) and refunds the allowance. The AI is a teacher pre-submission precisely so it can be a gate later without being hated.
    8 · HMs feel nothing. No nomination ever appears in an HM queue. Quality is proven on Paraform's attention, not the customer's.
