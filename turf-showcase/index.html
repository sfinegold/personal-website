Reflections from the campaign

Running technology & AI on a statewide race — poll analysis, internal tools, and a volunteer field app (Turf). What worked, and where AI fits.

Sam Finegold · Technology at Mahan for California — now consulting for campaigns & building Turf
Context

A startup campaign on a crunched timeline

  • The race: Governor of California, crowded field — Mahan entered ~3 months out with low name ID.
  • My role: brought in for tech & AI by the chairman, not the core team. Figured it out as we went — poll analysis → team use cases → pitched & built a field app.
  • The AI/tech "team": one paid employee (me) plus volunteers.

Workstream 1 · Analyze

AI-accelerated polling → ad targeting

  • AI was good at: cleaning & prepping data, writing model code, interpreting results, and shaping output files.
  • Human judgment stayed essential: which data to trust, how to combine poll methodologies, and pairing findings with the final ad copy.
  • Lesson: you're limited by your data — good polling is necessary — and you should structure questions to learn which message actually moves voters.
Structure polls to measure persuasion
Baseline ID→Show a message→Re-ask ID
The shift between the two IDs tells you which bio or ad copy is persuasive — then you run that as the ad.

AI in campaigns — my take

Where AI helps today — and where it doesn't

✅ Good at
Data analysis

Cleaning, modeling, and interpreting polling & field data — fast. Still needs human judgment on what data to trust and how to act on it.

◐ Potential
Campaign-lead workflows

Supporting the people running the campaign — e.g. the political lead sourcing and tracking endorsements.

⛔ Not there yet
Full ad production

End-to-end creative — the finished ad still needs human voice, taste, and sign-off.

The pattern: AI expands productivity on rote and analytical work — humans keep the judgment, the voice, and the final call.
